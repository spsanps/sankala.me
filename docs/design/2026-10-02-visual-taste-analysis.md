# What San's visual references say about him

October 2, 2026. Built from San's `Visual Taste.html` collection (references only;
the page's own styling was made by another AI and is not part of the brief), the
"Still Point" animation he shared, his September corrections in `AGENTS.md`, and
his existing work. San asked for this reading: why he likes these things, what he
would like next, and what he was trying to get at. Everything here is a hypothesis
for him to confirm or reject. None of it is a decision.

## What he keeps choosing

**1. Proof that a particular person was there.** Allysen's background is a 5.2-second
phone clip of light on her own wall (544×720, looping at 0.7×), not stock footage. Her
links are her reading list, her coffee map, her nearby parks and a letter to her younger
self. Sivers writes "Everything here is 100% me, no AI." Kate Sparkle's montage is
every X-Men character she has drawn over the years. Made in SF lists small named makers.
San's own corrections point the same way: "too AI-made", polished copy that "sounds
AI-written", a cover that made two days of work look like his identity, and weak images
used just to fill a slot. The objection is not to AI. It is to work with no person in it.

**2. Time made visible.** San named the loop as what Allysen got right. A still image
is a picture; a slow loop is a moment you are standing inside. The other references
also show duration. The montage compresses years of drawings into 97 seconds. The Still
Point holds one moment while its world keeps changing. A reading list is a year of
reading. Sivers lists his life in dated phases, and a /now page carries a date.

**3. Warmth and smallness.** Gold light, cream paper, peach backgrounds, glazed bowls,
watercolour pans, bunnies eating candy in space. The cold, dark, glowing, gradient-heavy
look of the AI industry (San's own field) is almost entirely absent. The two exceptions
are 21st and Impeccable, which are tools rather than taste.

**4. A strict frame around one living thing.** Small type, few links, one reading lane,
and one element that moves or varies.

**5. Craft as evidence of care.** Letterpress, screen printing, handmade watercolours.
San called the Fable video "a testament", and the part that impressed him was the labour:
about fifteen materials, each with its own process. When images can be generated in
seconds, visible effort is one of the few remaining signs that someone cared. That
explains the appeal of "thousands of lines for one frame".

## What may be underneath

- **San is an engineer who keeps making warm, handmade things.** ZINify turned research
  papers into zines. GPT-7 Will Have Arms became an illustrated essay with hand-built
  figures and then a painted film. He also made a bee simulation and a walkable O'Neill
  cylinder. His recurring move is translation: he takes something technical and makes it
  human, visual and playful. Lee Robinson's line "make technology easy to understand and
  interesting to learn about" describes that habit.
- **Code-drawn art is an engineer's way of working by hand.** Every mark is decided,
  editable, explainable and made under rules, much like chip layout at TI, where geometry
  obeys design rules. It may be the first medium where both halves of San's work are the
  same activity.
- **Open question:** the Paper Robots banner and film art come from image models. Does
  San feel about them the way visitors felt about the site ("too AI-made")? If so,
  replacing generated art with made art over time becomes a real goal for Paper Robots.
- **The site he wants is a lived-in place**, not a portfolio and not a campaign: a room
  with light coming in, his books and photographs, the people along the way, a letter,
  and what he is making now. It should be quiet enough that one moving thing makes it
  feel alive.
- **A wonder-struck streak**: the amateur astronomy club, robots, space habitats, the
  space bunnies. Paper Robots and Dyson Swarm carry this side of him.

## Predictions to test

If this reading is right, San will probably like most of these. The misses will
sharpen it.

- Bartosz Ciechanowski's interactive essays (mechanical watches, GPS, cameras): one
  engineer, pure code and hand-built figures that explain how things work.
- Inigo Quilez painting landscapes and characters with mathematics, and his process videos.
- Tiny Glade and Townscaper: cosy procedural worlds that feel handmade.
- Maggie Appleton's illustrated digital garden; Craig Mod's walking newsletters and books.
- Tom Sachs's Space Program: space missions rebuilt by hand in plywood, where space
  wonder meets visible handcraft.
- Wim Wenders's *Perfect Days*: a man who photographs light through leaves (komorebi),
  reads paperbacks and lives a small, deliberate life. It is close to Allysen's homepage
  as a film.
- Nicky Case's explorables: cute drawings that explain systems.

## What it means for the work

- **Divide the art by what is real.** For real parts of San's life (his room, Surathkal,
  the people), a real moment may be truer than code: his own short phone clip, slowed,
  behind a few lines of text, the way Allysen did it. Code-drawn art suits what a camera
  cannot capture: ideas, explanations, futures, robots and worlds. This matches the
  existing split, with real photographs and history on sankala.me and artwork on Paper
  Robots.
- **On sankala.me:** real moving moments, real photographs, and code-drawn figures that
  explain his ideas (the translation thread).
- **On Paper Robots:** made, code-drawn films and loops that can gradually replace
  generated art.
- **The quality bar:** the art should show its time through process, irregularity and a hand.

Samples testing this are in `design/prototypes/2026-10-code-drawn-art/`.

## San's response and the synthesis (same day)

San liked most of the samples ("lots of good ideas") but called Komorebi "just a literal
copy". It reproduced Allysen's surface (lowercase text over a light loop) instead of
transforming her idea. He asked for everything put together, closer to what he wanted.

What it adds up to: San wants his sites made the way the things he loves are made: by a
particular person, by hand, over time. A quiet frame holds one thing that is alive and
visibly cared for. Code is how an engineer makes things by hand, so the art should carry
his actual life (places, years, ideas) and move in real time. Real things stay real.

Three proposals (on the gallery page):
1. **Four desks** (sankala.me homepage and history, built as the synthesis prototype in
   `design/prototypes/2026-10-code-drawn-art/four-desks/`): one desk seen from the same
   spot in four places, each era in its own hand process (Surathkal cyanotype, Bengaluru
   engraving, San Diego risograph, San Jose in live colour on the real clock), with his
   real photographs pinned on the wall.
2. **Plates**: a monthly code-drawn plate (a /now in pictures), each in a process not
   tried before, collected in a strict grid and played as a montage after a year.
3. **Paper Robots, made not generated**: one robot skeleton in code, one hand process per
   film, with posters, thumbnails and loops from the same code.

Lesson for future work: transform a reference's idea; never reproduce its surface.
