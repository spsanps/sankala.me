# An audience-first home for San’s work

September 6, 2026. Recommendations for review, not approved platform setup.

San’s explicit direction: audience comes first; professional opportunities should
follow from the work and the people it reaches. San confirmed there is no existing
Substack; no newsletter or email list has been identified. Everything must remain
free and nonmonetized.

## Recommendation

Start one free Substack publication, provisionally **Paper Robots, by San Kala**.
Keep **sankala.me** as the author’s home and the permanent home of rich essays,
research notes, films with their sources, and experiments. Do not move or remove
the existing essay URLs. Use the name people already encounter on the films, with
San’s authorship explicit everywhere. The proposed Substack address has not been
checked or reserved.

Substack is worth a bounded trial because it combines email delivery with places
readers can discover a publication. Recommendations can appear in signup flows
and on publication pages, and report subscriptions attributed to recommendations.
That is a useful distribution facility; it does not mean merely opening an account
will create an audience. [Official Recommendations guide](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/5036794583828-How-can-I-recommend-other-publications-on-Substack).

Free publishing is available without charging readers. Keep paid subscriptions,
payment accounts, sponsorships, tips, affiliate links, and monetization out of
this setup. This follows San’s stated operating preference and is not a legal
assessment. [Substack’s free-publishing explanation](https://faq.substack.com/p/how-to-make-money-with-your-writing).

## What each place does

| Place | Its job | A reader’s useful next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sankala.me | The author’s home; illustrated essays, complete references, interactive work, research and contact | Read or watch a selected piece; subscribe if interested |
| Paper Robots on YouTube | Complete animated arguments and stories; a recognizable creative identity | Finish the film, read the full argument, or follow the next release |
| Paper Robots on Substack (proposed) | A useful letter by email, reader replies, Recommendations and occasional Notes | Subscribe by email or reply to a specific question |
| X | Self-contained thoughts, clips, diagrams, or demonstrations that introduce an idea | Go directly to the relevant film, essay, or letter |
| Dyson Swarm | The space collection: Another Sky and the swarm simulation | Explore, find San, and follow the same letter |

Avoid adding a compulsory site visit between an X post and a film someone wants
to watch. The right link depends on the post. The site brings the work together;
it does not need to intercept every click.

“Paper Robots” is currently the confirmed film identity. Extending it to the
newsletter and writing is a proposal. The prototypes show that relationship
without creating another named publication. Keep **San Kala** on author bylines,
the channel About, publication profile, and project credits so the work can lead
back to professional conversations.

## Should the essays move to Substack?

Keep the existing site essays. Start Substack with a useful edition of the
robotics argument and the published film, then selectively revisit the best
older pieces. Do not dump the archive into the feed all at once.

For a new substantial release:

1. Write the core argument or build the experiment. The website holds its
   complete, illustrated version, with references and updates.
2. Adapt that work into a letter that stands on its own: premise, one or two
   central insights, the film or image, and a meaningful question. Link to the
   rich site version for charts, citations, or the interactive experience.
3. Make a film when animation adds understanding or makes a story work. Each
   essay does not need a film before it can be published.
4. Extract a few things worth posting on their own: an argument, a striking
   scene, and a detail from making the piece. Use San’s normal voice.

There is no separate weekly newsletter obligation. A letter accompanying each
substantial release is a starting cadence, not a promised schedule.

Full-text publication in both places is also possible if it serves readers.
The tradeoff is keeping two copies accurate and deciding which page should
represent the piece in search. Do not rely on a supposed automatic “duplicate
content penalty,” or assume we can force Substack to set a cross-domain canonical
URL. Google treats canonical declarations as signals and can choose another
representative. Keep the site’s existing self-canonical URLs, permanent paths,
and clear authorship. [Google’s canonical URL guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

My starting recommendation is a complete, rich site edition plus a substantive
adapted letter. Reassess if Substack becomes the main place people actually read.

## What changes on the website

Lead with the work someone can immediately read, watch, or try. A resume-first
homepage asks a new reader to care about the author before seeing an idea.
The prototype instead pairs **GPT-7 Will Have Arms** with the film, then brings
forward **Another Sky** and the **EAI Challenge write-up**.

The artwork should carry the warmth and identity: actual gouache pieces on warm
paper, cobalt and vermilion, clear serif headings, and plenty of space. Navigation
and reading text should stay straightforward. The robot is a project mascot;
use San’s actual portrait for author and professional context.

Proposed structure, preserving all existing public routes:

- `/`: selected essays, latest film, one or two experiments, the free letter,
  short author context, and a clear work/background link.
- Existing `/essays/gpt7-will-have-arms`: same URL and full content; integrate
  film, essay, sources, and letter CTA into one readable experience.
- Existing `/notes`: a browsable writing and experiments index. A future filter
  can separate essays, films, and interactive worlds without creating empty pages.
- Existing `/resume`: keep the professional route and its useful details. A
  later `/about` or `/work` is optional, not necessary to launch the redesign.
- Proposed `/subscribe`: a stable site URL that leads to the chosen free email
  publication, once it exists. We can change the provider later without changing
  every older video description and profile link.

Use actual article titles, publication dates, author metadata, and preview
images. Keep the deployed site’s crawler article generation, canonical tags,
Open Graph cards, and accessible navigation intact during a future implementation.
Do not trade away the existing interactive essay for a prettier static excerpt.

## Discovery and learning

Try the publication for the next **three substantial releases**, then review the
effort and response. This is a proposed evaluation window, not a user deadline.

Keep a small release record: piece, date, title/thumbnail pair, where it was
shared, first-week subscribers gained where attributable, substantive replies,
returning readers/viewers, and relevant inbound conversations. Use comparable
windows. Record total reach too, but do not mistake a spike for a returning
audience or treat small samples as proof of a winning strategy.

The first film can teach us what attracted people and where they left. No
channel analytics or title/thumbnail test results have been inspected. It is
premature to promise a formula for going viral. Distinctive ideas, a clear
promise, a satisfying piece, and useful places to share it are things we can
work on; outcomes remain uncertain.

Two practical details:

- **Followers and email subscribers are different.** Substack followers can see
  Notes and app activity without being on the publication’s email list. Make the
  invitation about getting the next piece by email when that is the objective.
  [Subscriber dashboard guide](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058529871-How-do-I-use-the-subscriber-dashboard-on-Substack).
- **Keep an exportable list.** Substack supports CSV subscriber exports. Save a
  private backup periodically and keep it out of this public Git repository.
  Email portability reduces the cost of changing providers later; it does not
  make Substack’s entire recommendation or app audience portable.
  [Export guide](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/6314498343700-How-do-I-export-my-email-list-on-Substack).
